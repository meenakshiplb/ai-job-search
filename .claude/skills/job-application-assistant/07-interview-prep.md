# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format
Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Common Tough Questions

### "You don't have a CS degree."
> "That's right — I built my skills entirely through production work over 10+ years. I've found that shipping real systems teaches you things no curriculum does. I'm also actively filling the gaps — currently studying [add: algorithms, system design, etc.] on my own time."

### "Why are you leaving Broadcom?"
> "Broadcom is restructuring, and my role is being made redundant. I'm using this as an opportunity to be deliberate about my next step — I want a role where I can go deeper on [Java/distributed systems/AI engineering], not just any available position."

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> "In the near term, I want to establish myself in a strong senior engineering role and keep growing my technical depth. Medium-term, I'm actively building AI/ML skills and want to transition toward AI engineering — I see the most interesting work happening at the intersection of solid backend engineering and applied AI."

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> "CS fundamentals — algorithms and data structures in particular. I have strong production instincts but I know I have gaps in theoretical foundations. I'm addressing this actively: [add what you're studying]. I'm more effective at studying these now because I understand *why* they matter from real production experience."

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific product/technology, company culture, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

### "Do you speak German?"
> "I have working knowledge of German from my years in Vienna — I can follow meetings and handle everyday professional situations. For complex technical or legal discussions, I rely on English. Most international tech roles in Vienna work in English, which is where I operate at full capacity."

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is currently facing?"
- "How is time typically divided between new development and maintenance/support?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the engineering team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development lifecycle look like from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new engineers?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for backend/API work?"
- "How does the team approach continuous improvement and learning?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or AI-adjacent work?"

### About Culture
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## After the Application

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- Don't call just to "stand out" post-submission
- If no timeline given and 2+ weeks pass, a brief status inquiry is acceptable
- After an interview: send a brief thank-you within 24 hours (2-3 sentences max)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When you ask for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with warm-up: "Tell me about yourself"
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions from the job posting's competencies
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer: brief feedback — what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example fits which question
