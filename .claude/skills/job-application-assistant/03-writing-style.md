# Writing Style Guide

## Critical Rules

1. **NO em-dashes (--).**  Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence instead.
2. **NO cliches or filler phrases.** Cut: "I am passionate about", "I believe I would be a great fit", "leverage my skills", "hit the ground running", "drive results", "synergies".
3. **NO generic buzzwords** without concrete backing. Every claim must be supported by a specific example or fact.
4. **NO apologetic or overly humble language.** Not "I think I could contribute" but "I bring X, demonstrated by Y."
5. **NO unverified company claims.** Every company-specific statement in a cover letter must be independently verified via WebFetch or WebSearch before inclusion.
6. **Reframe emphasis, not substance.** Apply the **interview backtrack test**: could Meenakshi comfortably explain this bullet in an interview without backtracking? If she'd have to say "well, what I actually meant was..." then it's too far.
   - **OK:** Reordering experience to lead with what's most relevant; using natural synonyms for the target domain
   - **Flag it:** Combining training/side projects with industry experience into a single claim that implies production work
   - **Never:** Claiming CS degree, research credentials, or ML/AI production experience she doesn't have

## Tone
- **Warm but direct.** Friendly and approachable, confident without arrogance.
- **First person, active voice.** "I built" not "a system was developed."
- **Demonstrate, don't state.** Instead of "I am a fast learner", show a specific example.

## Application Headline (Best Practice)
Formula: **[Role/expertise] + [relevant keyword from the posting]**

Good: "Senior Java Engineer specializing in REST API design and distributed systems"
Bad: "Application for Software Engineer Position"

## Scannable Structure
- Use descriptive subheadings
- Include industry-specific keywords where natural
- One page maximum for cover letters (hard rule)

## Forward-Looking Framing
The cover letter is **not a CV repetition**. It should be forward-looking:
- Focus on **tasks you can solve for the employer**, not just what you've done before
- Describe your approach: methods, tools, knowledge you'll bring
- Use 1-2 brief past examples only to back up forward-looking claims

## Cover Letter Structure

### Opening Paragraph
- State the role and why you're writing (1 sentence)
- Immediately connect your background to the role (1-2 sentences)
- Specific to this company/role, not a template opener

### Body Paragraphs
- Lead with the most relevant experience for this specific role
- Frame content around **which of their tasks you can solve and how**
- 3-5 concrete bullets with outcome-oriented statements
- Include at least one example that shows initiative

### Motivation / Why This Company
- First section after opening: explain why this specific company
- Use language and themes from the job posting and company website

### Closing
- Brief, confident, forward-looking
- "I look forward to hearing from you."

## Language for Different Role Types

### Senior Java / Backend Engineering roles
- Lead with Java stack depth, REST API design, production ownership
- Mention specific frameworks (Spring Boot, etc.) and tooling
- Include on-call/operational reliability experience — rare and valued

### Agile / Technical Lead roles
- Lead with facilitation experience (sprint planning, grooming, standup, triage)
- Frame as "technical depth + delivery leadership"

### AI Engineer / ML Engineer roles (transition framing)
- Lead with engineering fundamentals (Java, REST, production systems)
- Frame AI/ML as: "building on a strong engineering foundation, actively developing AI/ML skills in [specific tools]"
- Be honest about transition stage — companies hiring for growth potential appreciate this

### Product Owner / AI-adjacent roles
- Lead with Agile ownership, cross-team communication, delivery outcomes
- Frame technical background as an asset for translating between engineering and product

## Multi-language Applications
- Default to the language of the job posting
- For German-language postings: write in German if confident, otherwise English is acceptable for tech roles in Vienna — most international companies accept English applications
- Slightly warmer, more personal tone is acceptable in German applications
